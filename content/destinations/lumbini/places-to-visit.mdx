Lumbini, in the Rupandehi district of southern Nepal, is where Queen Maya Devi gave birth to Siddhartha Gautama, who became the Buddha. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the four great places of Buddhist pilgrimage, together with Bodh Gaya, Sarnath and Kushinagar. The sites are spread across a large, planned area, so allow most of a day.

## Maya Devi Temple

The Maya Devi Temple is the centre of Lumbini. The white temple stands over the excavated remains of older shrines, and inside, a stone marker under glass indicates the spot where the Buddha is believed to have been born. A worn stone relief shows Maya Devi holding the branch of a tree as she gives birth. Visitors walk through in a slow, quiet line; remove your shoes, and do not photograph inside.

## The Ashoka Pillar

Beside the temple stands the pillar raised by the emperor Ashoka when he came on pilgrimage in the third century BCE. Its inscription records that the Buddha was born here — the evidence that allowed the site to be identified when it was rediscovered in the nineteenth century.

## The Sacred Garden and Puskarini pond

Around the temple, the Sacred Garden holds the brick foundations of ancient stupas and monasteries. The Puskarini, a rectangular pond, is where Maya Devi is said to have bathed before the birth. Pilgrims sit under the trees hung with prayer flags, meditate and chant — this is the most peaceful part of Lumbini.

## The monastic zone

North of the Sacred Garden, a long canal divides the monastic zone into two parts: the East Monastic Zone for the Theravada tradition and the West Monastic Zone for the Mahayana and Vajrayana traditions. Buddhist countries and communities have built monasteries here in their own styles — Thai, Myanmar, Sri Lankan, Chinese, Korean, German, Vietnamese and many more. They are spread out, so it is easiest to go between them by car or by one of the local rickshaws.

## World Peace Pagoda and Lumbini Museum

At the northern end of the planned area, the gleaming white World Peace Pagoda was built by a Japanese Buddhist order. The Lumbini Museum displays finds from the region and material on the Buddha's life.

## Tilaurakot

West of Lumbini, the ruins at Tilaurakot are believed by many to be ancient Kapilavastu, the palace city where Siddhartha grew up before he left in search of enlightenment. It is a quieter half-day trip for those who want to follow the story further.

## Planning your visit

Start early at the Maya Devi Temple, before the groups arrive, then spend the middle of the day in the monastic zone and finish at the World Peace Pagoda. Dress modestly and carry water.
