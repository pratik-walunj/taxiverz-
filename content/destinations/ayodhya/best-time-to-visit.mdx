Ayodhya is busy all year, but when you go makes a big difference — to the weather, to the length of the queues, and to what you will see on the ghats. Here is how the year looks.

## October to March: the pleasant months

The cooler, drier months from October to March are the most comfortable time to visit. Mornings are fresh, afternoons are warm but not harsh, and walking between the temples of the old town is a pleasure.

This is also when Ayodhya's biggest celebration happens. At Diwali, usually in October or November, the town holds Deepotsav: the ghats of Ram ki Paidi are lit with lakhs of earthen lamps, and the town is decorated for days. It is spectacular, and it is also the most crowded time of the year, with heavy traffic and restrictions on the roads near the river. If you plan to go for Deepotsav, book your car and your stay well ahead, and expect to walk.

In December and January, fog often covers the plains in the early morning and late evening. The highway between Gorakhpur and Ayodhya can slow to a crawl in thick fog, so leave after it lifts and avoid driving back late at night.

## March and April: Ram Navami

Ram Navami, the celebration of Ram's birth, falls in the Hindu month of Chaitra, usually in March or April. It draws very large crowds to the Ram Mandir and to the Saryu for a holy dip. If you want to be there for it, plan for long queues and a very early start; if you would rather avoid the crush, choose another week.

## May and June: hot

Summer is very hot, with strong sun and warm nights. Queues in the open are hard going in the middle of the day. If you visit in summer, go to the temples early in the morning, rest indoors in the afternoon, and return to the ghats in the evening, when the air cools a little by the river.

## July to September: the monsoon

The monsoon brings rain, humidity and a green landscape. In the Hindu month of Sawan, the Jhula Mela, when idols of Ram and Sita are placed on decorated swings in the temples, draws pilgrims to the town. Rain can be heavy, and the Saryu runs high; keep plans flexible and carry an umbrella.

## Our suggestion

For the most comfortable visit, go between late October and February, on a weekday, avoiding the days around Deepotsav and Ram Navami unless you want the festivals. Allow a full day, or stay overnight to see the evening aarti at Ram ki Paidi without rushing back.
