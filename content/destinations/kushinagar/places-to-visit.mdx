Kushinagar is where the Buddha, at the age of eighty, lay down between two sal trees and passed into mahaparinirvana. It is one of the four great places of Buddhist pilgrimage, with Lumbini, Bodh Gaya and Sarnath, and today it is a calm, green town whose sites are close together. Most visitors see them in half a day to a day.

## Mahaparinirvana Temple

The Mahaparinirvana Temple is the centre of Kushinagar. Inside lies a great reclining Buddha, carved from a single block of stone around the fifth century and about six metres long, showing the Buddha on his right side at the moment of passing. It was uncovered during excavations in 1876. Pilgrims from Sri Lanka, Thailand, Myanmar, Tibet and Japan come to offer robes and prayers, and the atmosphere inside is deeply quiet. Behind the temple stands the restored Nirvana Stupa, and around it the brick foundations of ancient monasteries.

## Ramabhar Stupa

A short drive from the temple, the Ramabhar Stupa marks the place where the Buddha's body was cremated. It is a large, grassy brick mound in a peaceful park, and many visitors walk around it in the late afternoon, when the light is soft.

## Matha Kuar shrine

The Matha Kuar shrine, near the main temple, holds a large statue of the Buddha seated in the earth-touching posture — the gesture of his enlightenment. It is smaller and less visited than the Mahaparinirvana Temple, which makes it a good place to pause.

## The international temples

Buddhist countries have built temples and monasteries in Kushinagar in their own styles. The Wat Thai temple has elegant Thai architecture and gardens; the Indo-Japan-Sri Lanka temple and the Chinese, Burmese and Tibetan monasteries are each worth a short stop. Walking or driving between them shows how far the Buddha's teaching travelled.

## Kushinagar Museum

The museum displays finds from the excavations in and around Kushinagar — sculptures, coins and pottery — and helps make sense of the ruins you have walked through.

## Planning your visit

The main sites are close together: the Mahaparinirvana Temple, the stupa behind it, the Matha Kuar shrine and the museum can be seen in a morning, with the Ramabhar Stupa and the international temples in the afternoon. Dress modestly, remove shoes where asked, and keep voices low inside the temples.

Kushinagar lies east of Gorakhpur along National Highway 27, and Kushinagar International Airport serves the town. Many pilgrims visit on a day trip from Gorakhpur, or combine it with [Lumbini](/destinations/lumbini/places-to-visit/) across the border in Nepal.
