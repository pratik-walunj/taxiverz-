Varanasi is a city of seasons as much as of ghats. The river rises and falls, festivals fill the steps with lamps, and the heat of summer changes the rhythm of the day. Here is how the year looks for a visitor.

## October to March: the best months

The months from October to March are the most comfortable for exploring. The air is cool, the river has settled back after the monsoon, and walking the ghats and the old lanes is easy. Mornings are the magic hours — a boat on the river at sunrise, mist over the water, pilgrims bathing on the steps.

This season also has the city's most beautiful festival. Dev Deepawali, on the full moon of the month of Kartik — usually in November, fifteen days after Diwali — sees the ghats lit with lakhs of lamps from end to end. It is unforgettable and very crowded: boats are booked long in advance, roads near the river are closed to traffic, and hotels fill early.

December and January are cold, with foggy mornings. On the river the fog can be beautiful; on the highways it is dangerous, and drives into or out of Varanasi can slow badly early and late in the day. Plan road journeys for the middle of the day in these months.

Mahashivratri, usually in February or March, brings huge crowds to Kashi Vishwanath and the Shiva temples; go for the festival or avoid those days if you want a quieter visit.

## April to June: hot

Summer in Varanasi is very hot, often above forty degrees in May and June. The ghats have little shade. If you visit in summer, keep to the early morning and the evening — a dawn boat ride and the evening aarti are still lovely — and rest indoors in the afternoon.

## July to September: the monsoon

In the monsoon the Ganga rises sharply. By August and September the water often covers many of the lower ghats, boat rides may be suspended when the current is strong, and the evening aarti is sometimes moved to higher ground. The city is green and far less crowded, and the sight of the swollen river is dramatic, but plans need to be flexible.

## Our suggestion

For a first visit, choose any time from late October to early March, avoiding the fog on long drives. Give yourself two or three days: one for the ghats and a dawn boat ride, one for Kashi Vishwanath and the lanes, and a half day for Sarnath. If you want to see Dev Deepawali, plan months ahead.
