Varanasi — also called Kashi and Banaras — is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, and for Hindus it is the holiest. Life here runs along the Ganga: more than eighty ghats line the western bank, and almost everything worth seeing is on the river or a short walk from it. Here is what to see, and how to pace a visit.

## The ghats

Walking the ghats is the essential Varanasi experience. Each has its own character: Dashashwamedh is the busiest, Assi at the southern end is where many visitors start their mornings, and between them are ghats for bathing, washing, wrestling practice and prayer. Manikarnika and Harishchandra are the cremation ghats, where fires burn day and night; you may watch respectfully from a distance, but do not photograph the cremations.

## A boat ride at dawn

The best way to see the ghats is from the river at sunrise, when pilgrims are bathing and the light turns the old buildings gold. Boats leave from most of the main ghats; agree the route and the price before you set off. An evening boat also gives a good view of the aarti.

## Ganga Aarti

Every evening at Dashashwamedh Ghat, priests perform the Ganga Aarti with lamps, bells and chanting — a ceremony watched by thousands, from the steps or from boats. Arrive well before sunset for a place. At Assi Ghat, a morning aarti and music programme, Subah-e-Banaras, welcomes the day.

## Kashi Vishwanath

Kashi Vishwanath, one of the twelve jyotirlingas of Shiva, is the city's most important temple. Since 2021 the Kashi Vishwanath Dham corridor has linked the temple to the river with wide courtyards, making it much easier to reach than through the old lanes. Phones and bags are not allowed inside; there are facilities to deposit them. Queues are longest early in the morning and on Mondays.

## Sarnath

A short drive from the city, Sarnath is where the Buddha gave his first sermon after his enlightenment. The Dhamek Stupa marks the spot; around it are the ruins of monasteries, the deer park, and the Mulagandha Kuti Vihar temple with its murals. The Sarnath Museum holds the Lion Capital of Ashoka, India's national emblem. Allow half a day.

## More to see

Banaras Hindu University has a large campus with the Bharat Kala Bhavan museum and the new Vishwanath temple. Across the river, Ramnagar Fort was the seat of the Maharajas of Banaras. The old lanes behind the ghats are full of silk weavers, sweet shops and small temples — and of Banarasi paan.

## Planning your visit

Two or three days let you see Varanasi without hurrying: a dawn boat ride and the ghats on the first day, Kashi Vishwanath and the lanes on the second, and Sarnath on the way out. Traffic near the river is heavy and cars cannot reach the ghats, so plan to be dropped nearby and walk.
